House Rules

The short version: tee off within a meter of the yellow marker, play the ball exactly where it lies, every attempt is one motion and one hit, and nobody scores worse than double par.

1 Teeing off

Start each hole with the ball on the ground within 1 meter of the yellow tee marker. Anywhere inside that circle is fair — in front, behind, or off to the side.

Your tee shot is hit number one on that hole.

2 Finishing a hole

Pick one mode before anyone tees off on hole 1, and play it the whole round so everyone's scores mean the same thing.

Easy mode Hit the tire. Any contact between your ball and the tire finishes the hole.
Normal mode Finish it one of three ways: the ball comes to rest in the tire, or your ball hits the pole, or your ball hits the cone. Rolling through the tire without staying in it doesn't count — play on from wherever it stops.

The hit that finishes the hole counts like any other. Mixed group? Nothing stops a younger player using easy mode — just agree up front.

3 Play it where it lies

You can never move the ball. Wherever it stops is where you play from — long grass, a slope, mud, up against a root. No nudging it to a better spot, no flattening what's around it, no kicking obstacles out of the way.

If the ball is genuinely unplayable — out of reach, wedged under something, in water, against a wall — you may place it at the nearest playable point, no closer to the hole, and add one penalty hit to your score.

  • Nearest, not nicest. Measure from where the ball actually sits and move the shortest distance that makes it playable.
  • The penalty is +1 on your card. Your next attempt from the new spot counts as a normal hit on top of that.
  • You decide whether your own ball is unplayable, but the group decides where it gets placed.

4 One motion, one hit

Every attempt must be a single continuous motion that touches the ball once. Within that, get creative:

  • A normal kick.
  • Drag it — pull or push the ball along with your foot, as long as it's one unbroken motion.
  • Scoop it — pick the ball up with your foot and flick it away, all in one motion.
  • Pinch and flick — trap the ball between both feet and flick it forward, again in one motion.

What breaks the rule:

  • Two separate kicks, or a touch to set it up before the real hit.
  • Juggling, dribbling, or stopping mid-motion and starting again.
  • Picking the ball up with your hands, or carrying or throwing it.

A whiff still counts as a hit. If you double-touch by accident, the attempt counts and you play the ball from wherever it ends up.

5 Scoring

Count every attempt, plus any penalty hits, and record the total for the hole.

The most you can score on any hole is double par — 8 on a par 4, 6 on a par 3, 10 on a par 5. Once you hit that number, pick the ball up, take the max, and move to the next tee. This app won't let you enter more.

Lowest total after all 18 holes wins.

6 Good form

  • Whoever is furthest from the hole plays next.
  • Never kick while someone is downrange — shout before you strike if you can't see.
  • Anything these rules don't cover, the group settles on the spot. It's the honor system out there.
